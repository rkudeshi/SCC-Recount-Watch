Santa Clara County · November 3, 2026Automatic Recount Watch

Under county policy, local contests decided by fewer than 25 votes or less than 0.25% of ballots cast get an automatic hand recount.

More info
“The Registrar of Voters shall conduct, prior to certification of election results, manual recounts in all contests wholly contained within Santa Clara County (excluding those for state and federal office) where the margin of victory is either less than 0.25 percent of the ballots cast, or less than 25 votes, except for countywide races or citywide races in the City of San Jose for which machine recounts may be used.”

Santa Clara County Board of Supervisors Policy 3.63, Automatic recounts in local races, adopted February 27, 2018 (Board Policy Manual, page 3-145, revised December 11, 2024). The Registrar of Voters explains how recounts work in Recounts and FAQs.

Margin is the votes for the last winning candidate minus the votes for the first losing candidate. For a ballot measure, it is the number of votes that would flip the outcome.

Why this page shows two percentages. The official results website shows each candidate's percentage of candidate votes, which counts only the people who voted in that contest. The recount policy uses ballots cast, which also counts people who skipped the contest (undervotes) or voted for too many (overvotes). Ballots cast for a contest is (votes + undervotes + overvotes) divided by the number of seats.

For example, say 40,000 ballots are cast in a contest and 8,000 voters skip it, so the candidates get 32,000 votes between them. If the margin is 90 votes, the official results website shows a gap of 0.28% (90 ÷ 32,000). As a share of ballots cast it is 0.225% (90 ÷ 40,000), which is under the 0.25% recount line. That is why this page shows both. (In vote-for-2 or vote-for-3 contests the ballots-cast percentage can be the larger one.)
